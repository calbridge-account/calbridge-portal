📖 Metrics Glossary

Definitions and formulas for every metric in your Calbridge dashboard.

Advertising Efficiency

Advertising Cost of Sales ACOS
ACOS = Ad Spend ÷ Ad-Attributed Sales × 100
The percentage of ad-attributed revenue that was spent on advertising. Lower is better — it means you're spending less to generate each dollar of ad-driven sales.
Example: $500 spend on $2,000 ad sales = 25% ACOS
💡 Compare ACOS to your break-even ACOS to know if a campaign is profitable.
Total Advertising Cost of Sales TACoS
TACoS = Total Ad Spend ÷ Total Revenue × 100
Unlike ACOS, TACoS measures ad spend against your total revenue — including organic sales. This gives the most honest picture of advertising efficiency for the whole business.
Example: $500 spend on $10,000 total revenue = 5% TACoS
💡 A declining TACoS over time means your organic sales are growing relative to ad spend — a sign of a healthy brand.
Return on Ad Spend ROAS
ROAS = Ad-Attributed Sales ÷ Ad Spend
How many dollars of ad-attributed revenue you get for every dollar spent. The inverse of ACOS. Higher is better.
Example: $2,000 sales on $500 spend = 4x ROAS
Break-Even ACOS
Break-Even ACOS = Gross Profit (CM2) ÷ Revenue × 100
The ACOS at which advertising neither profits nor loses money. If your actual ACOS is below this number, the campaign is profitable. Above it, you're losing money on ads.
Example: $8 CM2 on a $20 product = 40% break-even ACOS
💡 This is calculated automatically per ASIN once you upload your COGS.
Cost Per Click CPC
CPC = Total Ad Spend ÷ Total Clicks
The average amount paid each time a shopper clicks your ad.
Click-Through Rate CTR
CTR = Clicks ÷ Impressions × 100
The percentage of ad impressions that resulted in a click. A higher CTR usually indicates more relevant ads or better creative.
Example: 100 clicks on 10,000 impressions = 1% CTR

Profitability

Contribution Margin 1 CM1
CM1 = Revenue − FBA Fees − Referral Fees
The net cash Amazon deposits into your account per unit sold, before your own product costs. This is what Amazon actually pays you after taking their cut.
💡 For vendors: CM1 = the amount Amazon paid for your products (shipped COGS).
Contribution Margin 2 CM2
CM2 = CM1 − (Units Sold × Cost of Goods)
Gross profit per ASIN — what you make after Amazon fees and your own product cost, before advertising. Answers: "Is this product worth selling?"
💡 Requires COGS upload. If CM2 is negative, the product is unprofitable even without any advertising.
Contribution Margin 3 CM3
CM3 = CM2 − Direct Ad Spend
True profitability per ASIN after all costs including advertising. This is the most important metric for understanding which products are actually making you money. Answers: "Is advertising this product profitable?"
💡 If CM3 is negative, you are paying to lose money on this ASIN. Either reduce ad spend or improve the product margin.
FBA Fees
Fees Amazon charges for storing and fulfilling your products through Fulfillment by Amazon. Includes pick & pack, weight handling, and monthly storage fees. These are deducted before CM1 is calculated.
Referral Fee
Typically 8–15% of selling price (varies by category)
Amazon's commission on every sale, charged as a percentage of the total selling price. This is the largest fee for most sellers and is deducted before CM1 is calculated.

Sales & Volume

Ad-Attributed Sales
Sales revenue that Amazon attributes to an ad click within a defined attribution window (typically 7 or 14 days). A sale counts if the customer clicked your ad and purchased within that window — even if they didn't buy the advertised product.
💡 Attribution windows: SP uses 7 & 14 day click windows. SB uses 14 day only.
New-to-Brand Orders NTB
Orders from customers who have not purchased from your brand on Amazon in the past 12 months. NTB metrics show how effectively your advertising is growing your customer base vs. re-selling to existing customers.
💡 Available for Sponsored Brands campaigns only.
Same-SKU Sales
purchasesSameSku / purchases × 100
The percentage of ad-attributed purchases where the customer bought the exact product advertised (same SKU). Higher same-SKU rates mean your ads are converting on the specific product shown.
Top of Search Impression Share ToSIS
ToSIS = Your Top-of-Search Impressions ÷ Total Eligible Top-of-Search Impressions
The percentage of available top-of-search impressions your ads captured. Low ToSIS on important keywords means competitors are winning the most valuable placements.

Ad Types

Sponsored Products SP
Pay-per-click ads for individual products that appear in search results and product detail pages. The most common Amazon ad type. You bid on keywords or product targets and pay only when a shopper clicks.
Sponsored Brands SB
Banner ads featuring your brand logo, a custom headline, and multiple products. Appear at the top of search results. Best for brand awareness and driving traffic to your Store or a custom landing page. Uses 14-day attribution only.
Sponsored Display SD
Display ads that appear on and off Amazon — on product pages, Amazon-owned sites, and third-party sites. Can target audiences by shopping behavior or product/category. Supports both CPC and vCPM (cost per 1,000 viewable impressions) bidding.
Amazon DSP
Demand-Side Platform — programmatic display and video advertising that reaches Amazon audiences both on and off Amazon. Unlike Sponsored Ads, DSP is managed through Amazon's advertising platform and typically requires a minimum spend commitment. Uses CPM (cost per thousand impressions) billing.

Budget & Pacing

Budget Pacing
Pacing Ratio = Actual MTD Spend ÷ Expected MTD Spend
Whether a campaign is spending on track for the month. A ratio above 1.1 means over-pacing (at risk of exhausting budget early). Below 0.7 means under-pacing (budget may be wasted or campaigns are being throttled).
💡 Over-pacing campaigns often run out of budget in the afternoon, missing evening shoppers.
Dynamic Bids — Down Only
Amazon's default bidding strategy. Amazon may lower your bid when a click is less likely to convert, but will never raise it above your set bid.
Dynamic Bids — Up and Down
Amazon can raise your bid up to 100% (for top of search) or 50% (elsewhere) when a click is more likely to convert, and lower it when less likely. More aggressive and typically results in higher spend.
Fixed Bids
Amazon uses your exact bid for every auction — no automatic adjustments. Gives maximum control but misses optimization opportunities.

Targeting

Broad Match
Your ad can show for searches containing your keyword in any order, plus close variants and related terms. Widest reach, most discovery potential, but also the most irrelevant traffic risk.
Phrase Match
Your ad shows for searches containing your keyword phrase in the exact order, with additional words before or after. More targeted than broad but less restrictive than exact.
Exact Match
Your ad only shows for the exact keyword or very close variants. Most precise targeting — lowest reach but highest relevance. Usually highest conversion rate.
Negative Keywords
Search terms you explicitly exclude from triggering your ads. Critical for preventing wasted spend on irrelevant searches. Regularly reviewing search term reports to add negatives is one of the highest-ROI optimizations in Amazon advertising.
Auto Targeting
Amazon automatically targets relevant keywords and products based on your listing content. Good for discovery and finding new keyword opportunities, but requires careful monitoring to prevent wasted spend.

Performance Signals

Impression Share
Impression Share = Your Impressions ÷ Total Eligible Impressions
The percentage of available impressions your ads captured for a given keyword or placement. Low impression share means competitors are outbidding you or your budget is limiting delivery.
Search Term Impression Rank
Your ranking among advertisers competing for impressions on a specific search term. Rank 1 means your ads received the most impressions for that term. Available for Sponsored Brands keywords.
Detail Page Views DPV
The number of times shoppers viewed your product detail page within the attribution window after clicking or viewing an ad. A high DPV with low purchases may indicate a listing quality issue — price, images, or reviews.
Add to Cart ATC
Times a shopper added your product to their cart after an ad interaction. High ATC with low purchases suggests a checkout friction issue — price competitiveness, shipping cost, or availability.
Branded Searches
The number of times shoppers searched for your brand name within 14 days of an ad interaction. A growing branded search count indicates your advertising is building brand awareness and recall.
