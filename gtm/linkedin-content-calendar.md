## WEEK 1

### Post 1 (Monday) — Hook: counterintuitive take
> Most Amazon sellers are optimizing the wrong metric.
>
> ACoS gets all the attention. But ACoS doesn't tell you if you're making money.
>
> A 15% ACoS on a product with 18% margins after FBA fees and COGS means you're losing money on every sale — and your ads are accelerating it.
>
> The number that matters: contribution margin per ASIN.
>
> Revenue
> minus COGS
> minus FBA fees
> minus referral fees
> minus ad spend
> = what you actually made
>
> Run that math on your top 10 ASINs. At least 2 will surprise you.

## WEEK 2

### Post 4 (Monday) — Polarizing opinion
> Hot take: most Amazon agencies are accidentally working against their clients.
>
> Not on purpose. The incentive structure is just broken.
>
> You pay your agency a % of ad spend. Their incentive is to increase spend. Increasing spend increases ACoS. Higher ACoS gets justified as "brand awareness" or "launching keywords."
>
> Meanwhile your margin is getting compressed and nobody flagged it.
>
> The fix is simple: agency fees should be tied to profit metrics, not spend. Very few are willing to do this because it requires actually knowing the client's margin.
>
> We built tooling specifically so we could do this. It changes the whole conversation.

---

### Post 5 (Wednesday) — Practical tips
> 5 things I check every week for every client account:
>
> 1. Contribution margin trend (week over week, not month)
> 2. Any ASIN where ad spend > 40% of revenue
> 3. Campaigns with high impressions, low clicks — usually a bid or relevancy issue
> 4. Search terms converting at <50% of campaign average — negatives needed
> 5. Buy Box % on top 5 ASINs — drops here usually mean a pricing or inventory issue
>
> Most of these don't require fancy tools. They just require looking at the right data consistently.
>
> Which of these do you already track?
